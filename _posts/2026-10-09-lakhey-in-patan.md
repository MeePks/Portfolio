Lakhey is the demon dancer. In Patan we grow up with that mask. Big eyes, teeth out, a red mane. He looks like trouble. Hami Patan-baasi still bring him out into the tole, because the old story says he stays so he can look after the children.

There is more than one Lakhey. Majipa Lakhey is the Kathmandu one. He comes out for Indra Jatra, Yenya. The story there is simple. He wanted to live in the city, and he was allowed to stay if he protected the kids. That is why people call him a guardian.

In Patan the one we wait for is Mi Pwa Lakhey. Mi pwa means balls of fire. He is ours. Around Gathe Mangal he moves through the old lanes with fire. Musicians walk with him. The pyakhan can finish near Mangal Bazar, by Patan Durbar Square. The tole opens up, then fills in again behind him.

This dance had stopped for nearly thirty years. It is back in the lanes now. When Mi Pwa Lakhey comes out, people already know what to do. Give him room. Watch the fire. Stay for the chase.

The part the kids live for is the chase. They tease the Lakhey. They get close, they shout, and he turns. They run. There is a teaser in the dance, Jhyalincha, who starts it, and the other children join in. It is loud. It is funny. Then they come back, because nobody wants to miss the next round.

The mask is big and the steps are fast. People here say the Lakhey sits in the mask, and the dancer follows him. For a second the lane goes quiet. Then the drums come back in. You step aside. Same as any jatra in the tole. You do not have to be told.

I filmed Mi Pwa Lakhey in Patan so you can see that chase up close. The short is three of them in the lane. The other video is from the candle walk on Nhu Daya, our Newar new year, when the courtyards are full of lamps and he is still out there dancing.
