Payo Jatra happens in Patan Durbar Square during Dashain. Hami Patan-baasi usually just call it Payo. It is our jatra, in the old square, in the middle of the tole.

For about 24 years it did not happen. Dashain still came. The square was busy, families were home, and this jatra was simply not there. Now it is back, and it happens every year. The khadga comes out again each Dashain, and people come to Durbar Square for it.

{% include youtube.html id="k5DdPb-b29U" title="Payo Jatra in Patan Durbar Square vlog" %}

Khadga means the sacred sword. When the day comes, the square fills for the bearers. They carry the khadga through Durbar Square. They walk with their whole bodies trembling, and the crowd goes quiet around them. Many people here believe the deities themselves have stepped into the bearers. You feel that in how the tole makes space. You do not have to be told to step back. You just do.

The quiet is what you notice first. Then the khadga, and the bearers shaking as they walk through the square. It is Dashain, it is our tole, and the jatra is happening again. After so many years off, seeing the khadga bearers back every year still feels like something we got back.

One of the most fun moments was watching the Payo chase people around the square, especially the ones teasing them. The crowd would scatter, laugh, and gather right back again. One minute the square is still. The next minute people are running. Then they come back, because they want to see it again. The khadga is sacred, and the chase is part of the day too. The whole square joins in.

The Mayor of Lalitpur Metropolitan City was there too. I was happy to see him in the square with the khadga bearers. The jatra was gone for so long. Now it happens in Durbar Square every year, and the mayor was there with them.

I filmed the vlog so you can see the khadga bearers and that chase. If you have seen Payo Jatra in Patan, or a Payo in your own tole, tell me in the comments.
