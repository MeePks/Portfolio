## Culture and history

**What the name means.** Among Newars the festival is usually just called *Paya* or *Payo*. Historians still debate where the name comes from. One explanation, from cultural expert Indra Mali, is that it joins two Newari words: *pa*, a heavy, broad-bladed traditional sword, and *yaa*, a procession or jatra.

**Why it happens.** Payo is a victory procession. It celebrates good triumphing over evil through the legend of Goddess Durga defeating the demon Mahishasura. It falls on Dashain because Bijaya Dashami is believed to be the day the deities conquered evil. People believe the gods enter those who carry the khadga, which is why the bearers shake as they walk.

**When it happens.** Dashain follows the lunar calendar, and Bijaya Dashami is the tenth day of the bright fortnight (Shukla Paksha) of Ashwin. In 2026 that falls on **Wednesday, 21 October (Kartik 4, 2083 BS)**. In Patan, reports describe the jatra building up over Ashtami, Navami and Dashami, the eighth to tenth days of Dashain.

**How it unfolds in Patan.** Annapurna Post (2018) describes it like this:
- The khadgas come from Patan's main *agam ghar* (the secret Newar shrine house), called the *Mu Chhen*, in the Durbar Square area. Three bearers carry them, along with the main deity, and keep them in a room inside Taleju Bhawani's Mul Chowk.
- On **Maha Ashtami**, the three khadgas are carried to the **Mahalaxmi temple in Lagankhel** and installed there. That evening there is a puja, and a buffalo is sacrificed.
- On **Maha Navami**, the same bearers bring the khadgas back to Mul Chowk with music.
- On **Bijaya Dashami**, the **Navadurga troupe from Thecho** arrives. At the crossroads in **Mangal Bazaar**, the main deity, the three khadgas and the deities Bhairavi, Kali and Barahi are welcomed as forms of Goddess Durga. Then all the deities go back into Mul Chowk.

Kantipur reported that the revived jatra brought **Majudya (Manmaneshwari Devi)**, a Lichchhavi-era deity, together with the Thecho Navadurga three times in a ritual greeting. The dances of Bhairav, Kali and Barahi and the garlanding of Manmaneshwari Devi were the main attractions.

**Who keeps it alive.** According to Kantipur, Payo Jatra is the oldest of the Lichchhavi, Malla and Shah-era jatras linked to Patan Durbar Square. It is run by the **Mu Chhen Aangan Guthi**, a traditional Newar trust. Annapurna Post adds that by custom the khadga is carried by members of a particular Patan family line, which traces its descent to the Lichchhavis. The paper also credits the start of the tradition to the Lichchhavi king **Vasantadeva**. The *muhali* is played by a member of the Kapali community.

**A tradition that almost disappeared.** The jatra stopped for about 24 years. In 2018 (2075 BS), guthi members, Lalitpur Metropolitan City, the Lalitpur chamber of commerce, Newar journalists and local people revived it. Annapurna Post reported that one sword-bearer, Samrajyadev Shrestha of Mangal Bazaar, carried all three khadgas himself and spent about Rs 3 lakh of his own money on it. Things are still hard: the 2015 earthquake damaged the Mu Chhen agam ghar the procession sets out from, and the paper wrote that only one muhali player was left.

**Payo across the Valley.** Payo is not just a Patan tradition. Kathmandu neighbourhoods such as Te Bahal, Wotu, Ason and Maha Bouddha hold their own processions on Bijaya Dashami. Bhaktapur's Taleju holds a midnight *Payahdyo* (khadga deity) jatra. Lalitpur Metropolitan City's own heritage report also lists a Payaa procession at Kobahal in Lalitpur.

## Travel tips

- **Getting there:** Patan Durbar Square is in Mangal Bazaar, Lalitpur, a short ride south of Kathmandu across the Bagmati. [YOUR TIP: how you got there, e.g. bus, taxi or ride-share, and roughly how long it took.]
- **Best time to visit:** Dashain, especially Ashtami through Bijaya Dashami. In 2026 Bijaya Dashami is on 21 October. Jatra timings follow the lunar calendar and the guthis, so check a Nepali calendar such as [Hamro Patro](https://www.hamropatro.com) and local news that week. Thecho's Navadurga dancers also perform in Mul Chowk on Kojagrat Purnima (the full moon that ends Dashain).
- **Entry fee / cost:** The Nepal Tourism Board lists entry to Patan Durbar Square (including Patan Museum) at NPR 1,000 for foreign nationals and NPR 500 for SAARC/BIMSTEC nationals. For Nepali visitors the fee applies only to the museum. Check the current fee at the gate.
- **Don't miss:** The Navadurga dancers from Thecho, the khadga bearers, and Mul Chowk itself, the palace courtyard surrounded by the Taleju temples.
- **Be respectful:** This is a living religious ritual, not a show. Give the procession and the khadga bearers room, follow instructions from volunteers and guthi members, and ask before photographing people up close. Expect animal sacrifice during Dashain rituals.
